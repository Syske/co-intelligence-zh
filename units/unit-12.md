## 本单元讲了什么

本单元紧接单元 11 的"幻觉"话题顺势推进：AI 无法轻易胜任要求精确与准确的关键任务（mission-critical），但幻觉也让 AI 能在训练数据之外找到新颖关联——"AI 创造力的悖论"：让 LLM 在事实性工作上不可靠甚至危险的同一个特性，恰恰让它们变得有用。随后进入"自动创造力"（Automatic Creativity）一节，作者先颠覆自动化的历史规律：从蒸汽动力到机器人，枯燥、重复、分析性的任务总是最先被自动化；但 LLM 极擅长写作，Transformer 底座延伸到艺术、音乐和视频生成，于是受这波 AI 冲击最大的，反而是创造性任务最多的工作。人们的不安源于把"新奇"（novelty）误认为"原创"（originality）——新想法不凭空而来，创新学者早就指出"重组"（recombination）才是想法生成的关键，莱特兄弟（自行车修理工经验 × 鸟类飞行观察 → 可控飞机）正是"第一个看见概念之间关联的人"。由此引出全书核心比喻之一：LLM 是"关联机器"（connection machines）——在词元间建立人眼看不出、却具更深层含义的关系，加上输出随机性，即成创新工具。作者亲自示范：把"快餐、熔岩灯专利、14 世纪英格兰"三个烂点子扔给 AI，得到"熔岩灯郡"（Lava Lampshire）中世纪主题快餐店的完整企划。心理学测验呈现"量的碾压"：替代用途测验（AUT）中人类两分钟典型想出 5–10 个牙刷新用途，AI 想出 122 个；Haase & Hanel 的盲评实验显示 GPT-4 的创意力超过除 9.4% 之外的所有人类；远距离联想测验（RAT）中"关联机器"通常满分。但作者在结尾留下隐忧：这些创意是否原创很难说清——AI 不检索数据库、靠训练中的关联生成，出处不可追溯，可能涉及版权、专利或未经许可的风格取用（"毕加索风格"问题），图像生成为甚；艺术与意义的讨论留待后文。

## 英文原文

**AI as a Creative — Automatic Creativity**

…wholly relying on LLM-generated answers. That said, we need to be realistic about a major weakness, which means AI cannot easily be used for mission-critical tasks requiring precision or accuracy. Hallucination does allow the AI to find novel connections outside the exact context of its training data. It also is part of how it can perform tasks that it was not explicitly trained for, such as creating a sentence about an elephant who eats stew on the moon, where every word should begin with a vowel. (The AI came up with: An elephant eats an oniony oxtail on outer orbit.) This is the paradox of AI creativity. The same feature that makes LLMs unreliable and dangerous for factual work also makes them useful. The real question becomes how to use AI to take advantage of its strengths while avoiding its weaknesses. To do that, let us consider how AI "thinks" creatively.

**Automatic Creativity**

Given the history of automation, many people would have predicted that the first tasks AI would be good at would be boring, repetitive, and analytical. Those are usually the first tasks to be automated in any wave of new technology, from steam power to robots. As we have seen, however, that is not the case. Large Language Models are excellent at writing, but the underlying Transformer technology also serves as the key for a whole set of new applications, including AI that makes art, music, and video. As a result, researchers have argued that it is the jobs with the most creative tasks, rather than the most repetitive, that tend to be most impacted by the new wave of AI.

This tends to make us uncomfortable: After all, how can AI, a machine, generate something new and creative? The issue is that we often mistake novelty for originality. New ideas do not come from the ether; they are based on existing concepts. Innovation scholars have long pointed to the importance of recombination in generating ideas.

Breakthroughs often happen when people connect distant, seemingly unrelated ideas. To take a canonical example, the Wright brothers combined their experience as bicycle mechanics and their observations of the flight of birds to develop their concept of a controllable plane that could be balanced and steered by warping its wings. They were not the inventors of the bicycle, the first to observe birds' wings, or even the first people to try to build an airplane. Instead, they were the first to see the connections between these concepts. If you can link disparate ideas from multiple fields and add a little random creativity, you might be able to create something new.

LLMs are connection machines. They are trained by generating relationships between tokens that may seem unrelated to humans but represent some deeper meaning. Add in the randomness that comes with AI output, and you have a powerful tool for innovation. The AI seeks to generate the next word in a sequence by finding the next likely token, no matter how weird the previous words were. So it should be no surprise that the AI can come up with novel concepts with ease. I asked AI to: Find me business ideas that would incorporate fast food, patent 6,604,835 B2 [which turned out to be for a lava lamp that included bits of crystal], and 14th century England.

It suggested:

A medieval-themed fast-food restaurant that serves burgers, fries, and shakes in a castle-like setting, with staff dressed as knights, princesses, and jesters. The restaurant could use patent 6,604,835 B2 to create decorative lava lamps with gems or coins floating in them, to add some sparkle and ambiance to the dining area. The lava lamps could also be sold as souvenirs or gifts to customers. This idea could appeal to families with children, history buffs, or anyone looking for a fun and unique dining experience.

You may hate this idea (or love it, depending on your tolerance for lava lamp–centric restaurants), but it somehow made sense out of the three unrelated ideas I gave it, and if I didn't like that one, it would be happy to generate many more. And while I am not sure I want to quit my job to start Lava Lampshire, as the AI suggested I name my restaurant, this sort of response does exhibit a level of creativity. In fact, by many of the common psychological tests of creativity, AI is already more creative than humans.

One such test is known as the Alternative Uses Test (AUT). This measures an individual's ability to come up with a wide variety of uses for a common object. In this test, a participant is presented with an everyday object, such as a paper clip, and is asked to come up with as many different uses for the object as possible. For example, a paper clip can hold papers together, pick locks, or fish small objects out of tight spaces. The AUT is often used to evaluate an individual's ability to think divergently and to come up with unconventional ideas.

You can try the AUT now: Come up with creative ideas for using a toothbrush that does not involve brushing your teeth. Make them as different from each other as possible. You have two minutes. I'll wait.

Time's up.

How many did you come up with? Between 5 and 10 is a typical number. I asked an AI to do exactly the same task and it came up with 122 ideas in two minutes (and the version of AI that I used is likely far slower than what is available when you are reading this book). And while some ideas do share similarities ("use it as a brush to get dirt off mushrooms" and "use it as a tool to brush dirt off fruits"), there are also a lot of interesting ideas, from sculpting fine textures in frosting to applying it as a miniature drumstick ("perfect for a dollhouse drum set").

Are these original ideas? It is often very hard to tell. The AI is not explicitly searching a database of ideas; instead, it relies on its training to find connections, some of which certainly existed before. In my web searches, I found one 1965 picture of a man from Scotland playing a cake tin with toothbrushes—but there is no way to know whether that was part of the training of the AI or not. This is part of the concern about using AI for creative work; since we can't easily tell where the information comes from, the AI may be using elements of work that might be copyrighted or patented or just taking someone's style without permission. This is especially true for image generation, where it is very possible for an AI to closely reproduce a work "in the style of Picasso" or "inspired by Banksy" that has many of the features of the artist without any of the human meaning behind it. We will return to this question of art and meaning later, but it is worth considering a more subjective standard: Do we think the output of AI art is original, compared to what a human can do?

A recent paper by Jennifer Haase and Paul Hanel did just that, having humans blindly judge the creativity of AIs compared to humans in the AUT. After testing AI and 100 people on various objects, ranging from balls to pants, they found the GPT-4 model outperformed all but 9.4 percent of humans tested in generating creative ideas, as judged by other humans. Given that GPT-4 was the latest model tested, and it was much better than previous AI models, it might be expected that the creativity of AIs could continue to grow over time.

Of course, there are other creativity tests as well. A popular one is the remote associates test (RAT). This test asks people to find the common word that connects a set of three seemingly unrelated words. For example, pine, crab, and sauce are connected by the word apple. (Try one: What word connects cream, skate, and water? The AI got it right.) Unsurprisingly, as a connection machine, AI usually maxes out this test, too.

## 中文翻译

**作为创造者的 AI —— 自动创造力（Automatic Creativity）**

……完全依赖 LLM 生成的答案。话虽如此，我们仍需对一项重大短板保持清醒，这意味着 AI 无法轻易用于要求精确性或准确性的关键任务（mission-critical tasks）。幻觉确实能让 AI 在训练数据的确切语境之外，找到新颖的关联。它也是 AI 能够执行那些未经显式训练的任务的部分原因——比如写一个关于大象在月球上吃肉炖菜的句子，且每个词都必须以元音开头。（AI 给出的句子是：An elephant eats an oniony oxtail on outer orbit.「一头大象在外层轨道上吃着洋葱味的牛尾」。）这就是 AI 创造力的悖论：同一个特性，既让 LLM 在事实性工作上不可靠甚至危险，也让它们变得有用。真正的问题于是变成了：如何利用 AI 的长处，同时避开它的短处？为此，让我们先来看看 AI 是如何"创造性地思考"的。

**自动创造力**

回顾自动化的历史，许多人大概会预测：AI 最先擅长的任务会是枯燥、重复且分析性的。从蒸汽动力到机器人，在每一波新技术浪潮中，这类任务通常总是最先被自动化的。然而，如我们所见，事实并非如此。大语言模型极擅长写作，但底层的 Transformer 技术同时也是一整套新应用的关键，包括创作艺术、音乐和视频的 AI。因此，研究者们提出：受这波 AI 新浪潮冲击最大的，往往是创造性任务最多的工作，而非重复性最多的工作。

这难免让我们不安：毕竟，一台机器怎么能生成崭新而有创造性的东西？问题在于，我们常常把新奇（novelty）误认为原创（originality）。新想法不会从以太（the ether）中凭空涌出；它们都建立在既有概念之上。创新研究学者早就指出，重组（recombination）在想法生成中的重要性。

突破往往发生在人们把遥远的、看似无关的念头连接起来之时。举一个经典的例子：莱特兄弟将自己身为自行车修理工的经验，与对鸟类飞行的观察结合起来，发展出了可控飞机的概念——机翼可以通过扭转变形来保持平衡并实现转向。他们既不是自行车的发明者，也不是最早观察鸟类翅膀的人，甚至不是最早尝试制造飞机的人。相反，他们是第一批看见这些概念之间关联的人。如果你能把来自多个领域的不同想法联系起来，再添加一点点随机的创造力，你或许就能创造出某种新东西。

LLM 是"关联机器"（connection machines）。它们的训练，就是在词元（tokens）之间生成关系——这些关系在人类看来可能毫不相干，却代表着某种更深层的含义。再注入 AI 输出中固有的随机性，你就得到了一件强大的创新工具。AI 生成序列中下一个词的方式，是寻找下一个最可能的词元——无论前面的词有多么古怪。所以，AI 能轻松地想出新颖的概念，也就不足为奇了。我曾让 AI：给我找一些商业创意，把快餐、专利 6,604,835 B2［后来查明，这是一个内含水晶碎屑的熔岩灯专利］和 14 世纪的英格兰结合起来。

它建议：

一家中世纪主题的快餐店，以城堡般的场景供应汉堡、薯条和奶昔，员工装扮成骑士、公主和小丑。餐厅可以利用专利 6,604,835 B2 制作装饰性熔岩灯，让宝石或硬币悬浮其中，为就餐区增添几分闪耀与氛围。这些熔岩灯还可以作为纪念品或礼物卖给顾客。这个创意可能吸引带着孩子的家庭、历史爱好者，或是任何想获得有趣而独特用餐体验的人。

你也许讨厌这个创意（也可能喜欢它，取决于你对"以熔岩灯为中心"的餐厅的容忍度），但它确实把我给的三个互不相干的点子拼出了某种意义；而如果我不满意，它很乐意再生成很多个。虽然我并不确定自己想辞掉工作，去创办"熔岩灯郡"（Lava Lampshire）——这是 AI 给我建议的餐厅名字——但这类回答确实展现出了一定程度的创造力。事实上，在许多常见的心理学创造力测验中，AI 已经比人类更有创造力。

其中一项测验叫做"替代用途测验"（Alternative Uses Test, AUT）。它测量一个人为一个普通物件想出多种多样用途的能力。在这项测验中，被试会拿到一个日常物品，比如一枚回形针，然后被要求尽可能多地想出它的不同用途。例如，回形针可以把纸夹在一起、撬锁，或者把卡在狭缝里的小物件勾出来。AUT 常被用来评估一个人发散思维（think divergently）和提出非常规想法的能力。

你现在就可以试试 AUT：想出一些使用牙刷的创意用法，前提是不用来刷牙。让它们彼此之间尽可能不同。你有两分钟。我等着。

时间到。

你想出了几个？5 到 10 个是典型的数字。我让 AI 做完全相同的任务，它在两分钟内想出了 122 个点子（而我用的这个版本的 AI，很可能远慢于你读这本书时能够用到的版本）。虽然有些点子确实彼此相似（"用它当刷子清除蘑菇上的泥土"和"用它当工具刷掉水果上的污垢"），但也有不少有趣的点子——从在糖霜上雕刻精细的纹理，到把它当迷你鼓槌用（"简直是娃娃屋架子鼓的绝配"）。

这些是原创的点子吗？往往很难说清。AI 并不是在显式地检索一个点子数据库；相反，它依赖自己的训练来寻找关联，其中一些关联毫无疑问此前就已存在。在我的网络搜索中，我找到了一张 1965 年的照片：一位苏格兰男士用牙刷敲击蛋糕铁盒演奏——但无从知晓那是否属于 AI 训练数据的一部分。这正是将 AI 用于创作工作的顾虑之一：由于我们不容易辨别信息究竟来自何处，AI 可能正在使用某些受版权（copyrighted）或专利（patented）保护的作品元素，或者只是在未经许可的情况下取用某人的风格。在图像生成领域尤其如此——AI 很有可能高度仿制出"毕加索风格"或"受班克斯启发"的作品，具备了这位艺术家的诸多特征，却不含任何背后的人类意义。艺术与意义的问题我们稍后再谈，但这里值得考虑一个更主观的标准：与人类所能做的相比，我们认为 AI 艺术的产出是原创的吗？

Jennifer Haase 和 Paul Hanel 最近的一篇论文正是做了这件事：让人类在盲评中比较 AI 与人类在 AUT 中的创造力。在测试了 AI 和 100 个人、物品从球到裤子各不相同之后，他们发现：就"由其他人类评判"的创意生成而言，GPT-4 模型超过了除 9.4% 之外的所有受试人类。考虑到 GPT-4 是当时受测的最新模型、且远胜于以往的 AI 模型，可以预期 AI 的创造力或许会随时间持续增长。

当然，创造力测验还有其他种类。流行的一种是"远距离联想测验"（Remote Associates Test, RAT）。这项测验要求人们找出一个共同的词，把一组三个看似无关的词连接起来。例如，pine（松树）、crab（蟹）和 sauce（酱）由 apple（苹果）一词相连。（试一个：什么词连接 cream（奶油）、skate（滑冰）和 water（水）？AI 答对了。）毫不意外，作为一台关联机器，AI 通常也能在这项测验中拿满分。

## 核心观点

1. **AI 创造力的悖论**：AI 无法轻易胜任要求精确与准确的关键任务（mission-critical tasks），但幻觉让它能在训练数据之外找到新颖关联，也是它执行未经显式训练任务的部分原因（"月球大象元音句"）——同一特性既让 LLM 不可靠又有用；真正的问题是如何扬长避短。
2. **自动化历史规律被逆转**：以往枯燥、重复、分析性任务最先被自动化（蒸汽机到机器人皆然）；但 LLM 极擅长写作，Transformer 底座延伸到艺术、音乐、视频生成——研究者提出：受冲击最大的是创造性任务最多的工作，而非重复性最多的工作。
3. **新奇 ≠ 原创**：人们对"机器怎能创造"的不安，源于把 novelty（新奇）误认为 originality（原创）；新想法不凭空而来，创新学者早已指出重组（recombination）是想法生成的关键。
4. **莱特兄弟：重组式突破的经典样本**：自行车修理工经验 × 鸟类飞行观察 → 可控飞机（扭转变形机翼）；他们不是任何部件的首创者，而是第一个看见概念之间关联的人。
5. **LLM 是"关联机器"（connection machines）**：训练即在词元间建立人眼看不出、却具更深层含义的关系；加上 AI 输出的随机性，即成强大创新工具——无论前文多古怪，都继续寻找最可能的下一词元。
6. **三个烂点子的合成创意**："快餐 + 熔岩灯专利 + 14 世纪英格兰" → "熔岩灯郡"（Lava Lampshire）中世纪主题快餐店完整企划——AI 从无关输入中拼出意义，且不满意可无限再生成。
7. **心理学测验上的"量的碾压"**：替代用途测验（AUT）中，人类两分钟典型想出 5–10 个牙刷新用途，AI 两分钟想出 122 个；Haase & Hanel 的盲评实验显示 GPT-4 的创意力超过除 9.4% 之外的所有人类受试；远距离联想测验（RAT）中，作为关联机器的 AI 通常满分。
8. **原创性困境与版权阴影**：AI 不检索数据库、靠训练中的关联生成，出处不可追溯——可能使用受版权/专利保护的元素，或未经许可取用他人风格；"毕加索风格"问题（有艺术家的特征、无背后的人类意义）在图像生成中尤为突出；艺术与意义的讨论留待后文。
