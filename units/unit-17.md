## 本单元讲了什么

承接上单元"任务与系统"的框架，本单元把镜头对准任务层：任务在锯齿边界上的位置、以及边界本身的变化都需要时间和经验，因此必须坚持原则一"邀请 AI 参与一切"，在学习边界形状的过程中，有意识地决定把哪些任务交给 AI——目标是"少做无聊的工作"的同时保持"人在回路"。为此作者给出任务三分类的第一类"只归我的任务"（Just Me Tasks），它其实包含两种截然不同的情况：一是 AI 目前只会添乱的任务（讲笑话——AI 的"戴领结的鱼"冷笑话让作者直言"我的话就摆在这儿"），但要记住原则四"这是最差的 AI"，短板是暂时的；二是你出于个人或伦理理由坚决不愿外包的任务（抚养孩子、重大决策、表达价值观），随着 AI 变强，后一类将越来越重要。作者以身说法：本书几乎全部写作就是 Just Me Task，理由有三——AI 写不出个人风格（暂时）、写作过程本身帮助思考、AI 产出的版权归属在法律上不明；他还演示了让 AI 克隆自己文风、代笔"为何不外包写作"的段落，反讽地道出这恰是他想说的第二点。第二类是"委派任务"（Delegated Tasks）：交办并核查、但不想多花时间，理想的是"人类乏味重复、AI 轻松高效"的事（报销单、医疗表格、整理邮件、订航班），可以很复杂、并非零风险，且越委派越难核查（报税、投资、健康诊断），"在驾驶座上睡着"正是隐患；作者分享了本章唯一一次委派——让 AI 总结 Dell'Acqua 论文，靠"熟悉该领域所以能核查大改"的底气省了 30 分钟，还请本尊盲审获认可。最后是第三类"自动化任务"（Automated Tasks）：完全交权、不予检查，目前只是极小类别——AI 犯错太多，除非其他系统能强制保证答案的准确性（下单元续）。

## 英文原文

### 6 AI AS A COWORKER (continued)

...where tasks are placed on this frontier and how the frontier will change. That takes time and experience, which is why it is important to stick with the principle of inviting AI to everything, letting us learn the shape of the Jagged Frontier and how it maps onto the unique complex of tasks that comprise our individual jobs. With that knowledge, we need to be conscious about the tasks we are giving AI, so as to take advantage of its strengths and our weaknesses. We want to be more efficient while doing less boring work, and to remain the human in the loop while also addressing the value of AI. To do this well, we need a framework, where we divide our tasks into categories that are more or less suitable for AI disruption.

#### Tasks for Me, Tasks for AI

At the level of tasks, we need to think about what AI does well and what it does badly. But we also need to consider what we do well and what tasks we need to remain human. Those we can call Just Me Tasks. They are tasks in which the AI is not useful and only gets in the way, at least for now. They might also be tasks that you strongly believe should remain human, with no AI help. As AI improves, the latter category likely becomes more important than the former. For example, AI is currently terrible at jokes, unless you absolutely love dad humor. (Don't take my word for it. I asked it to tell me a joke to disprove this assertion, and the AI responded: "I think AI can be funny sometimes. Here's a joke for you: What do you call a fish that wears a bowtie? So-fish-ticated." . . . I rest my case.) So joke writing would be a Just Me Task, because it is something the AI cannot do. But remember the fourth principle: this is the worst AI you will ever use. By the time you read this, AI comedians may have become very good. Which brings us to the second type of Just Me Task: Do we even want AI to write jokes?

We have already seen that there is not a clear bright shining line of "human things" that the AI cannot do. It does a good job approximating empathy, creativity, and humanity. Trying to find things that AI can definitely not do because they are uniquely human may ultimately be challenging. But that doesn't mean we want AI to do all these things. We may reserve Just Me Tasks for personal or ethical reasons, such as raising our children, making important decisions, or expressing our values.

Almost all the writing in this book is a Just Me Task. There are three reasons for this. First, the AI is good at writing, but not that good at writing with a personal style. I think, or fear, that is likely a temporary situation. I can already get a pretty good approximation of my tone by working with the AI. To see how that works, I gave the AI the text of this chapter, up until this sentence, and asked it: Describe my writing style. It told me that my writing is a blend of academic rigor, personal insight, and practical advice, presented in a conversational, accessible manner.

It is always nice to get a flattering analysis, but now I can go further, asking the AI to clone my tone and approach: Continuing in this style, write me a paragraph about why an author may not want to delegate writing to an AI, even if it is a good writer. And the AI responds:

While it's true that AI has made impressive strides in writing capabilities, there are compelling reasons why an author might choose to keep their pen (or keyboard) firmly in hand. For one, writing is an intensely personal process. It's a way to bring unique insights, experiences, and voice to the page. Each sentence we write is imbued with our individuality and perspective, creating a connection with the reader that is uniquely human. Delegating this task to an AI, no matter how sophisticated, could risk losing that personal touch.

Furthermore, the act of writing can be a journey of self-discovery, an opportunity to clarify our thoughts, and a way to engage deeply with our subject matter. By handing over the reins to AI, we could potentially miss out on these enriching experiences. While AI can undoubtedly assist in many ways, it's essential to remember that it is a tool—a tool that can enhance our capabilities, but not replace the distinctively human qualities that make our writing truly our own.

Not bad. And it was exactly the second point I was going to make. Delegating the task to an AI, no matter how sophisticated, could risk losing that personal touch, and the process of writing helps us think. Or whatever the AI said.

The third reason I won't delegate my writing to an AI is the delicate issue of copyrights and the law. Right now, it is unclear whether the AI's output is protected by copyright. This is one of many policy decisions that will greatly shape the development of AI, and policies are likely to evolve over time. Indeed, as a society, Just Me Tasks are not going to be static; they can change as AI evolves and as preferences shift. The key is to recognize the tasks that are meaningful and fulfilling for you as a human being and that you would rather not delegate or share with an AI system.

The next category of tasks is Delegated Tasks. These are tasks that you assign the AI and may carefully check (remember, the AI makes stuff up all the time), but ultimately do not want to spend a lot of time on. This is usually stuff you really don't want to do and is of low importance, or time-consuming. The perfect Delegated Task is tedious, repetitive, or boring for humans but easy and efficient for AI. Delegated Tasks are not necessarily simple or straightforward; they can be very complex and sophisticated. They are also not risk-free; they can have serious consequences if done incorrectly or maliciously by the AI system. Think about the expense reports and health forms that you have to deal with, or other tasks like sorting your emails, scheduling your appointments, or booking your flights. You will still check over the results and confirm they are right, though this may become harder, especially as AI improves and you might wish to delegate some tasks that are beyond your expertise or interest, such as filing your taxes, managing your investments, or diagnosing your health problems. And it becomes even more challenging when falling asleep at the wheel is a concern. The future of delegation will require further reductions in hallucination rates, and better transparency of AI decision-making, so that we can trust it more. The whole goal of delegation is to save us time and allow us to focus on tasks where we can be, or want to be, of value.

I made significant changes to the output of the AI, but delegating this task, rather than rereading and summarizing the paper myself, probably saved me thirty minutes. ……哪些任务被摆在这条边界之上，以及这条边界又将如何变化。这需要时间和经验，而这也正是为什么我们必须坚持"邀请 AI 参与一切"的原则——让我们得以了解锯齿边界的形状，以及它如何映射到构成我们各自工作的那组独特任务之上。有了这样的认识，我们就需要有意识地选择交给 AI 的任务，以便利用它的长处和我们的短处。我们想要更高效，同时少做无聊的工作；我们想继续做"回路中的人"，同时也不辜负 AI 的价值。要把这件事做好，我们需要一个框架，把任务划分为或多或少适合被 AI 颠覆的几类。

#### 我的任务与 AI 的任务

在任务层面，我们需要思考 AI 擅长什么、不擅长什么。但我们也需要考量：我们自己擅长什么，以及哪些任务必须继续由人来完成。后者我们可以称之为"只归我的任务"（Just Me Tasks）。在这些任务上，AI 帮不上忙，至少目前只会添乱。它们也可能是你强烈认为应该保留给人、不应有任何 AI 参与的任务。随着 AI 进步，后一类很可能会变得比前一类更重要。例如，AI 目前讲笑话讲得很糟糕——除非你特别喜欢老爸式冷笑话。（别只听我说。我让 AI 讲个笑话来推翻这个论断，它回答："我觉得 AI 有时也挺好笑的。给你讲一个：你怎么称呼一条戴领结的鱼？So-fish-ticated（谐音 sophisticated，'有派头的'）。"……我的话就摆在这儿。）所以，写笑话就会是一项"只归我的任务"，因为那是 AI 做不到的事。但请记住第四原则：这是你将使用过的最差的 AI。等你读到这里时，AI 喜剧演员可能已经非常出色了。这就引出了第二类"只归我的任务"：我们真的想让 AI 来写笑话吗？

我们已经看到，并不存在一条清晰闪亮的"人类专属"界线，规定 AI 绝对不能做某些事。它在模仿共情、创造力和人性方面都做得不错。想要找出一些 AI 绝对做不了、因为那是人类独有的事，最终可能相当困难。但这并不意味着我们希望 AI 去做所有这一切。我们可能出于个人或伦理原因保留一些"只归我的任务"——比如抚养我们的孩子、做出重要的决定，或表达我们的价值观。

本书几乎全部的写作，就是一项"只归我的任务"。原因有三。第一，AI 擅长写作，但并不那么擅长写出个人风格。我想——或者说担心——这很可能只是暂时的局面。通过与 AI 合作，我已经能得到一个相当接近我语气（tone）的仿作。为了看看这是怎么做到的，我把本章写到这里之前的文本交给 AI，然后问它：描述我的写作风格。它告诉我，我的写作"融合了学术严谨、个人洞见与实用建议，以对话式、平易近人的方式呈现"。

再者，写作的行为可以是一段自我发现的旅程，一次厘清思绪的机会，一种与所写主题深度博弈的方式。把缰绳交给 AI，我们可能错过这些丰富的体验。AI 固然能在许多方面提供帮助，但务必记住：它是一个工具——一个能增强我们能力的工具，却不能替代那些使我们的写作真正属于我们自己的、鲜明的人类特质。

## 核心观点

1. **先画边界，再分任务**：任务在锯齿边界（Jagged Frontier）上的位置、以及边界本身的变化，都需要时间和经验——所以要坚持原则一"邀请 AI 参与一切"，在学习边界形状的过程中有意识地分配任务，利用 AI 的长处补我们的短处，做到"更高效 + 少做无聊的工作"，同时保持"人在回路"（human in the loop）。
2. **"只归我的任务"（Just Me Tasks）有两个子类**：① "AI 不能（至少目前）"——如写笑话："你怎么称呼一条戴领结的鱼？So-fish-ticated"。但记住原则四：这是最差的 AI，短板是暂时的。② "我们不愿"——出于个人或伦理理由坚决不外包：抚养孩子、重大决策、表达价值观。AI 已能很好地模仿共情、创造力和人性，"人类绝对做不了"的清单越来越难列；随 AI 变强，"不愿"会比"不能"越来越重要。
3. **作者不外借本书写作的三个理由**：① AI 写不出个人风格（他担心这只是暂时的——AI 已能相当接近地克隆他的文风）；② 写作过程本身帮助思考、是自我发现的旅程（AI 代笔的"为何不外包写作"恰好命中他自己想说的第二点，略带反讽）；③ AI 产出的版权保护在法律上尚不明确。"只归我的任务"不是静态的，会随 AI 演化与观念变迁而改变。
4. **"委派任务"（Delegated Tasks）**：交办 + 核查，但不想多花时间。理想形态：对人类乏味、重复、无聊，对 AI 轻松高效（费用报表、医疗表格、整理邮件、安排约会、预订航班）。三个要点：① 未必简单，可以非常复杂精密；② 并非零风险——做错或恶意执行的后果可能很严重；③ 越委派越难核查——报税、投资、健康诊断这类超出专业/兴趣的任务，会放大"在驾驶座上睡着"的风险。委派的未来取决于幻觉率下降与决策透明度提升。
5. **一次成功委派的完整样本**：作者本章唯一委派 AI 的任务，是总结同事 Dell'Acqua 的论文——因为熟识其工作，才有信心核查并大改 AI 摘要，省了约 30 分钟；随后电邮本尊"盲审"（未透露 AI 参与），获认可并小改定稿。启示：委派的底气来自"可核查的领域熟悉度"，而非对 AI 本身的信任。
6. **"自动化任务"（Automated Tasks）**：完全交权、不予检查（如某类邮件全交 AI）。目前只是极小类别：今天的 AI 犯错太多，无法自动化使用；只有当其他系统强制保证 AI 答案的准确性时，情况才会开始改变（下单元续）。
