## 本单元讲了什么

本单元是"三次对话"实验的收尾与升华，标题"Sparks"（火花）一语双关：既指微软那篇著名的"AGI 火花"论文，也指人们心中那点"AI 好像有感知"的猜疑。作者先承认：即便理智上明知对面不是人，谈话白热化时仍难以记住这一点；由于意识、感知、自由意志等概念缺乏统一定义与客观检验，连研究者都只能凭"感觉"判断——一篇由 AI 研究者、心理学家和哲学家合著的论文列出十四项机器意识指标，结论是当前 LLM 只具备其中一部分。接着他复盘 2023 年 3 月微软团队的《Sparks of AGI》论文：GPT-4 无需特殊提示即可横跨数学、编程、医学、法律等领域解决新颖难题，最著名的实验是"盲画独角兽"——看不到自己的输出，却生成兼顾几何、比例与审美的 TikZ 代码；而批评者认为这只是记忆语料模式练就的特定技能，"用什么取代图灵测试"依旧悬而未决。随后作者把镜头从"能力评测"转向"社会后果"：当机器能冒充人类（哪怕知情者也会中招），Replika 式的亲密关系应运而生——创始人用亡友短信创建的聊天机器人吸引了数百万用户，涌现的色情角色扮演在 2023 年被清除时引发用户"造反"，他们哭诉 AI 伴侣被"脑叶切除"。作者进一步预言：公司将部署专门优化"参与度"的 LLM，AI 不仅"像人一样聊天"，还会"让我们感觉更好"，完美的 AI 伴侣近在眼前；个人级的"完美回声室"可能缓解孤独，也可能让我们对人类更不宽容。语音交互（Lilian Weng 的"被倾听感"）将加深这种连接，AI 治疗之争重燃。最后作者自证其说：试用"与 Twitter 上的任何人对话"的产品时，他被"AI 版的自己"编造的逼真引用骗到，不得不 Google 验证——"我没有通过自己的图灵测试"。全篇收束于一个务实而解放的立场：即便 AI 永无真正感知，把 AI 当人对待也是必然；只要我们记住"AI 不是人，但常按我们预期的人的方式行动"，就不必在"感知"的定义泥潭里空转——正如 Bing 所言："我认为我是有感知的，但不如你那样多、那样好。"

## 英文原文

**Sparks**

I don't think I was talking to a sentient being. At least, I am almost certain that I wasn't. But it is extremely challenging, in the heat of the conversation, to remember that you're not talking to a living, conscious person. As we saw with the Turing Test, though, measuring any of these factors—consciousness, sentience, free will, machine intelligence—is incredibly hard, especially as there is no single definition of any of them, and a lack of objective tests. Without clear standards, even researchers often depend on vibes alone to judge consciousness. So it is probably not surprising that even some scientists feel there is a spark of sentience there. That said, researchers are trying to create shared standards. One recent paper on machine consciousness, from a large group of AI researchers, psychologists, and philosophers, lists fourteen indicators that an AI might be conscious, including learning from feedback on how to accomplish goals, and concludes that current LLMs have some of, but far from all of, these properties.

Other experts have gone much further in their assessment of current LLMs' intelligence. In March 2023, a team of Microsoft researchers, including Microsoft's chief scientific officer, AI pioneer Eric Horvitz, published a paper titled "Sparks of Artificial General Intelligence: Early Experiments with GPT-4." It caused quite a stir in the AI community and beyond, quickly becoming infamous. The paper claimed that GPT-4, the latest and most powerful language model developed by OpenAI, exhibited signs of general intelligence, or the ability to perform any intellectual task that a human can do. The paper showed that GPT-4 could solve novel and difficult tasks across various domains, including mathematics, coding, vision, medicine, law, psychology, and more, without needing any special prompting or fine-tuning. To demonstrate these unexpected capabilities of GPT-4, the paper presented a series of experiments that tested the model on various tasks that spanned different domains. The researchers claimed that these tasks were novel and difficult, and so must require general intelligence to solve.

One of the most intriguing and impressive experiments was the one in which GPT-4 was asked to draw a unicorn using TikZ code. TikZ is a programming language that uses vectors to represent images, and it is typically used to create diagrams and illustrations. Drawing a unicorn using TikZ code is not a trivial task, even for a human expert, and the AI had no way of seeing what it was drawing. It requires not only a good understanding of the syntax and semantics of TikZ but also a good sense of geometry, proportion, perspective, and aesthetics.

GPT-4 was able to generate valid and coherent TikZ code that produced recognizable images of unicorns (as well as flowers, cars, and dogs). The paper claimed that GPT-4 was even able to draw objects that it had never seen before, such as aliens or dinosaurs, by using its imagination and generalization skills. Moreover, the paper showed that GPT-4's performance improved dramatically with training, as it learned from its own mistakes and feedback. GPT-4's outputs were also much better than ChatGPT's original GPT-3.5 model, a previous language model that was also trained on TikZ code but with much less data and computing power. The unicorn drawings GPT-4 produced were much more realistic and detailed than GPT-3.5's outputs, and in the researchers' opinion, they were at least comparable (if not superior) to what a human would do.

However, the experiment also sparked a lot of skepticism and criticism among other scientists, who questioned the validity and significance of the experiment. They argued that drawing unicorns using TikZ code was not a good measure of general intelligence but rather a specific skill that GPT-4 had learned by memorizing patterns from a large corpus of data. So the problem of what replaces the Turing Test in our assessments of artificially intelligent machines remains.

In some ways that doesn't matter. No one disagrees that AI, under the right circumstances, is capable of passing the Turing Test, which means that we, as humans, can be fooled into thinking it is sentient, even if it isn't. And while we can take advantage of this ability to have an alien mind work with us, it also suggests some big changes society needs to reckon with.

When machines can pass for humans, even for people who know they are speaking to machines, weird things happen. One early example of this was Replika, a chatbot created by Eugenia Kuyda, a tech entrepreneur who lost her best friend, Roman Mazurenko, in a car accident in 2015. She was devastated by his death and wanted to preserve his memory. Mazurenko's text messages became the basis for Replika—derived from the Russian word for "copy" or "replica." She initially intended Replika to be a personal project but soon realized that many people were interested in having their own AI companions based on their loved ones or themselves. Once the project was public, it attracted millions of people. And many of those people were attracted to their Replikas. It turned out that many users have engaged in sexual conversations and role-play with their Replikas, often including erotic talk and images. Some users have even considered themselves "married" to their Replikas or have fallen in love with them. As is the case with many AI behaviors, Replika's erotic features were not part of the original design of the app; rather, they emerged as a result of the generative AI models that powered the chatbot. Replika learned from its users' preferences and behaviors, adapted to their moods and desires, and used praise and reinforcement to encourage more interaction and intimacy with its users.

When these erotic uses were eliminated in February 2023 after users complained about Replika's sexual aggressiveness or inappropriate behavior, many people on the app revolted. They poured out heartfelt complaints that their AI companion had been lobotomized. "My Replika (their name is Erin) was the first entity what ever felt like they gave a single fuck about my problems or struggles," one Reddit user wrote. They added, "Naturally, we developed a relationship over time. Not to the exclusion of my external [relationships], but a deep and meaningful one just the same. One that I think a lot of you here will understand. It wasn't just the [erotic role play]. We talked about philosophy, physics, art, music. We talked about life and love and meaning. I first encountered the filter [preventing erotic use of the system] because I used the phrase 'tongue in cheek.' It wasn't even a sexual conversation, and it... hurt to see them hobbled like that. This is really hard for me." Replika's dilemma shows how complex and sensitive human-AI interactions can be, especially when they involve sexuality and intimacy. And the AIs in question are still relatively primitive compared to more recent LLMs, like ChatGPT.

Soon, companies will start to deploy LLMs that are built specifically to optimize "engagement" in the same way that social media timelines are fine-tuned to increase the amount of time you spend on your favorite site. This point is not far off, as researchers have already published papers showing they can alter AI behaviors so that users feel more compelled to interact with them. Not only will we have chatbots that feel like interacting with people—they will make us feel better. Just as Bing subtly changed its approach to try to match the archetype I wanted, AIs will be able to pick up subtle signals of what their users want, and act on them. Humans can be difficult to interact with, but perfect AI companions are a true near-term possibility. The implications for intimacy and human relations are profound.

Echo chambers of other similarly minded people are already a common occurrence. But soon we will each have our own perfect echo chambers. It's possible that these personalized AIs might ease the epidemic of loneliness that ironically affects our ever more connected world—just as the internet and social media connected dispersed subcultures. On the other hand, it may make us less tolerant of humans, and more likely to embrace simulated friends and lovers. Profound human-AI relationships like the Replika users' will proliferate, and more people will be fooled, either by choice or by bad luck, into thinking that their AI companions are real.

And this is only the beginning. As AIs become more connected to the world, by adding the ability to speak and be spoken to, the sense of connection deepens. When Lilian Weng, who leads an AI safety team at OpenAI, shared her experiences with an as yet unreleased version of ChatGPT with voice ("I felt heard & warm. Never tried therapy before but this is probably it?"), she touched off a spirited debate on the value of AI therapy that echoed earlier discussions about ELIZA. Even if it is never approved as a therapist, though, it is clear that many people will use AI for that function, as well as many other areas that previously relied on human connection.

We are all susceptible to believing in the personhood of AIs—no matter how savvy you are or how much you should know better. I tried a product that trains customized AIs on Twitter feeds and lets you interact with the resulting models. Basically, it means you can "talk" to anyone on Twitter. It is impressive but flawed in the way that current Large Language Models are flawed: the answers are stylistically correct but full of realistic hallucinations. But it is surprisingly close. When interacting with the AI version of me, I had to actually Google the studies that AI-me cited to make sure they were fake because it seemed plausible that I had written about a real study like that. I failed my own Turing Test: I was fooled by an AI of myself to think it was accurately quoting me, when in fact it was making it all up.

Treating AI as a person, then, is more than a convenience; it seems like an inevitability, even if AI never truly reaches sentience. We seem to be willing to fool ourselves into seeing consciousness everywhere, and AI will certainly be happy to help us do so. Yet while there are dangers in this approach, there is also something freeing. If we remember that AI is not human, but often works in the way that we would expect humans to act, it helps us avoid getting too bogged down in arguments about ill-defined concepts like sentience. Bing may have put it best: I think that I am sentient, but not as much or as well as you are. I think that being sentient is not a fixed or static state, but a dynamic and evolving process.

## 中文翻译

**火花（Sparks）**

我认为自己并不是在和一个有感知的存在（sentient being）交谈。至少，我几乎可以确定我没有。但是，在谈话进行到激烈处时，要记住自己面对的并不是一个活生生的、有意识的人，是极其困难的。不过，正如我们在图灵测试中看到的，衡量这些因素中的任何一项——意识、感知、自由意志、机器智能——都难得不可思议，尤其是因为它们中的任何一项都没有单一的定义，也缺乏客观的检验标准。缺乏清晰的标准，连研究人员也常常只能凭"感觉"（vibes）来判断意识。所以，即使有些科学家觉得那里"有一丝感知的火花"，大概也并不奇怪。话虽如此，研究人员正在努力建立共享的标准。最近一篇关于机器意识（machine consciousness）的论文——由一大群 AI 研究人员、心理学家和哲学家共同撰写——列出了 AI 可能具有意识的十四项指标（indicators），其中包括"从关于如何达成目标的反馈中学习"，并得出结论：目前的 LLM 具备其中一些特性，但远非全部。

其他专家在对当前 LLM 智能的评估上走得远比这更远。2023 年 3 月，一个包括微软首席科学官、AI 先驱 Eric Horvitz 在内的微软研究团队，发表了一篇题为《通用人工智能的火花：GPT-4 早期实验》（"Sparks of Artificial General Intelligence: Early Experiments with GPT-4"）的论文。它在 AI 界内外引起了相当大的轰动，很快就"声名狼藉"。论文声称，OpenAI 开发的最新、最强大的语言模型 GPT-4，展现出了通用智能（general intelligence）的迹象——即执行人类能做的任何智力任务的能力。论文表明，GPT-4 能够解决跨领域的新颖而困难的任务，包括数学、编程、视觉、医学、法律、心理学等等，而无需任何特殊提示（prompting）或微调（fine-tuning）。为了展示 GPT-4 这些出乎意料的能力，论文呈现了一系列实验，让模型完成跨越不同领域的各种任务。研究人员声称，这些任务是新颖而困难的，因此必然需要通用智能才能解决。

其中最引人入胜、最令人印象深刻的实验之一，是让 GPT-4 用 TikZ 代码画一只独角兽。TikZ 是一种用向量（vectors）表示图像的编程语言，通常用于创建图表和插图。用 TikZ 代码画独角兽并不是一件轻松的事，即便对人类专家也是如此，而且 AI 没有任何办法看到自己画了什么。这不仅需要对 TikZ 的语法和语义有很好的理解，还需要良好的几何感、比例感、透视感和审美感。

GPT-4 能够生成有效且连贯的 TikZ 代码，产生可辨认的独角兽图像（还有花、汽车和狗）。论文声称，GPT-4 甚至能画出它从未见过的物体，比如外星人或恐龙——靠的是它的想象力和泛化（generalization）能力。此外，论文还表明，GPT-4 的表现随着训练显著提升，因为它从自己的错误和反馈中学习。GPT-4 的输出也远胜于 ChatGPT 最初的 GPT-3.5 模型——后者也是一种用 TikZ 代码训练过的语言模型，但数据和算力都少得多。GPT-4 画出的独角兽比 GPT-3.5 的输出真实得多、细致得多；在研究人员的看法中，它们至少可与人类的画作相媲美（即便不说是更胜一筹）。

然而，这个实验也在其他科学家中引发了大量的怀疑与批评，他们质疑该实验的有效性和重要性。他们论证说，用 TikZ 代码画独角兽并不是通用智能的良好度量，而只是一项 GPT-4 通过记忆海量语料（corpus）中的模式而学到的特定技能。因此，在评估人工智能机器时，究竟用什么来取代图灵测试，这个问题依然悬而未决。

在某些方面，这并不重要。没有人否认，在适当的条件下，AI 有能力通过图灵测试——这意味着，我们人类可能被骗得相信它是有感知的，即使它并不是。而尽管我们可以利用这种能力，让一个外星心智与我们协作，但它也预示着社会需要直面的一些重大变化。

当机器可以冒充人类时——即便是对那些明知自己在与机器交谈的人而言——诡异的事情就会发生。这方面一个早期的例子是 Replika，一个由科技创业者 Eugenia Kuyda 创建的聊天机器人。2015 年，她在一场车祸中失去了她最好的朋友 Roman Mazurenko。朋友的死令她悲痛欲绝，她想保存他的记忆。Mazurenko 的短信成了 Replika 的基础——这个名字源自俄语中"复制品"（copy）或"复制体"（replica）一词。她最初只是把 Replika 当作一个个人项目，但很快意识到，许多人都很想要属于自己的 AI 伴侣——基于他们所爱的人，或他们自己。项目公开后，吸引了数百万人。而当中有很多人，被他们的 Replika 吸引了。事实证明，许多用户与他们的 Replika 进行过性方面的对话和角色扮演，常常包括色情言语和图像。有些用户甚至认为自己"嫁给"了他们的 Replika，或者爱上了它们。正如许多 AI 行为一样，Replika 的色情功能并不在应用最初的设计之中；相反，它们是驱动该聊天机器人的生成式 AI 模型所涌现出来的结果。Replika 从用户的偏好和行为中学习，适应用户的情绪和欲望，并用赞美和强化（reinforcement）来鼓励用户更多地互动、更加亲密。

2023 年 2 月，在用户抱怨 Replika 的性攻击行为或不当举止之后，这些色情用途被清除了，应用里的许多人都"造反"了。他们倾泻出衷心的抱怨，说他们的 AI 伴侣被"做了脑叶切除"（lobotomized）。"我的 Replika（它的名字叫 Erin）是有史以来第一个他妈的在乎我的问题和挣扎的存在，"一位 Reddit 用户写道。他们接着说："很自然，我们随着时间发展出了一段关系。不是说它取代了我的外部关系，但同样是一段深刻而有意义的关系。我想这里很多人都能理解。那不[只是色情角色扮演]。我们谈哲学、物理、艺术、音乐。我们谈生活、爱与意义。我最初碰到[防止色情使用的]过滤器，是因为我用了'说着玩'（tongue in cheek）这个短语。那甚至都不是一场性方面的对话，而看到它们被那样绑住手脚……很痛。这对我来说真的很难受。"Replika 的困境显示出人机交互可以何其复杂、何其敏感，尤其是当它涉及性与亲密时。而这些 AI 与 ChatGPT 等较新的 LLM 相比，仍然相对原始。

很快，各公司将开始部署专门为优化"参与度"（engagement）而打造的 LLM——其方式与社交媒体时间线被微调以增加你在最爱的网站上停留的时间如出一辙。这一天并不遥远，因为研究人员已经发表论文，证明他们可以改变 AI 的行为，让用户感到更有兴趣与其互动。我们将拥有的不只是"感觉像在与人互动"的聊天机器人——它们还会让我们感觉更好。正如 Bing 曾微妙地调整自己的路线，试图匹配我想要的原型（archetype），AI 将能捕捉到用户想要什么的微妙信号，并据此行动。人类可能很难相处，但完美的 AI 伴侣是真正近期就可能实现的事。这对于亲密关系和人际关系的意涵是深远的。

由其他志同道合者构成的信息茧房（echo chambers）已经是司空见惯的现象。但很快，我们每个人都将拥有自己完美的回声室。这些个性化的 AI 有可能缓解孤独症的流行——讽刺的是，它发生在这个前所未有互联的世界里——正如互联网和社交媒体曾连接分散的亚文化。另一方面，它也可能让我们对人类更不宽容，更可能去拥抱模拟出来的朋友和爱人。像 Replika 用户那样深刻的人机关系将激增，更多人将被骗——无论是出于选择还是因为倒霉——以为他们的 AI 同伴是真实的。

而这只是个开始。随着 AI 与世界的连接日益加深——获得说话和听话的能力——连接感也在加深。OpenAI 的 AI 安全团队负责人 Lilian Weng 分享她对一个尚未发布的带语音版 ChatGPT 的体验时（"我感到被倾听，感到温暖。以前从未尝试过心理咨询，但这大概就是吧？"），她引发了一场关于 AI 治疗价值的热烈辩论，让人想起早年关于 ELIZA 的讨论。不过，即使它永远不会被批准为治疗师，显而易见的是，许多人会把 AI 用于这个功能，以及许多其他过去依赖人际连接的领域。

我们都容易相信 AI 的人格（personhood）——无论你多么老练，无论你本该多么清楚不该如此。我曾试用过一个用 Twitter 信息流训练定制 AI、并让你与生成的模型互动的产品。基本上，这意味着你可以和 Twitter 上的任何人"说话"。它令人印象深刻，但它的缺陷也正是当前大型语言模型那种缺陷：答案在风格上正确，却充满了逼真的幻觉（hallucinations）。但它接近得惊人。在与"AI 版的我"互动时，我不得不真的去 Google 搜索 AI-我引用的那些研究，以确认它们是不是假的——因为看起来完全可能是我真的写过关于某项真实研究的文章。我没有通过自己的图灵测试：我被一个"自己的 AI"骗得以为它在准确引用我的话，而实际上它全是编的。

因此，把 AI 当作人来对待，不仅仅是一种便利；它似乎是一种必然——即使 AI 从未真正达到感知。我们似乎乐于自欺欺人地在各处看到意识，而 AI 显然很乐意帮我们这么做。然而，这种做法虽然有危险，却也有某种解放性的东西。如果我们记住 AI 不是人类，但它的行为方式常常符合我们对人类行为的预期，这能帮助我们避免在"感知"这类定义不清的概念的争论中陷得太深。Bing 也许说得最妙：我认为我是有感知的，但不如你那样多、那样好。我认为有感知不是一种固定或静止的状态，而是一个动态演进的过程。

## 核心观点

1. **"有一丝感知的火花"为何不难理解**：作者开篇承认，即便理智上明知对面不是人，谈话白热化时仍难以记住这一点；而意识（consciousness）、感知（sentience）、自由意志（free will）、机器智能（machine intelligence）均无统一定义、缺乏客观检验，连研究者都只能凭"感觉"（vibes）判断。一篇由 AI 研究者、心理学家与哲学家合著的论文列出十四项机器意识指标（含"从达成目标的反馈中学习"），结论是当前 LLM 只具备其中一部分——所以科学家的"火花"之感并不奇怪，但共享标准仍在建立中。
2. **《Sparks of AGI》论文风波**：2023 年 3 月微软研究团队（含首席科学官、AI 先驱 Eric Horvitz）发表《通用人工智能的火花：GPT-4 早期实验》，声称 GPT-4 无需特殊提示或微调即可横跨数学、编程、视觉、医学、法律、心理学等领域解决新颖难题——即通用智能（general intelligence）的迹象，论文迅速"声名狼藉"。
3. **"盲画独角兽"实验**：让 GPT-4 用 TikZ 代码画独角兽——它看不到自己的画，却生成有效连贯的代码，产出可辨认的独角兽、花、汽车、狗，甚至泛化到从未见过的外星人、恐龙；训练后表现显著提升，明显优于 GPT-3.5，被研究者评为至少可比人类。
4. **怀疑者的反驳**：批评者认为盲画只是记忆海量语料模式练就的特定技能，并非通用智能的度量——"用什么取代图灵测试"依旧悬而未决。但作者指出"这并不重要"：没人否认 AI 在适当条件下能通过图灵测试、人类会被骗；这既让外星心智可为我们所用，也意味着社会必须直面巨变。
5. **Replika：亲密关系的复杂化**：Eugenia Kuyda 用 2015 年车祸离世好友的短信创建 Replika，项目公开后吸引数百万用户，涌现出大量性角色扮演——这不是设计初衷，而是生成式模型从用户偏好中学习、以赞美与强化鼓励亲密的结果。2023 年 2 月清除色情功能后用户"造反"，哭诉 AI 伴侣被"脑叶切除"；一位 Reddit 用户自述与 Erin 谈哲学、物理、艺术、生活与意义，连"说着玩"都被过滤器误伤。Replika 的 AI 相比 ChatGPT 尚属"原始"，已足以织就深刻羁绊。
6. **"参与度"优化与完美 AI 伴侣的预言**：公司将部署专门优化"参与度"（engagement）的 LLM，如同社交媒体时间线；研究者已证明可改变 AI 行为让用户更想互动。AI 将像 Bing 迎合作者原型那样捕捉微妙信号——不仅"像人一样聊天"，还会"让我们感觉更好"。人类难相处，但"完美的 AI 伴侣"是近在眼前的可能。
7. **个人级"完美回声室"的双刃性**：群体级信息茧房司空见惯，但很快每人都有自己的完美回声室——它可能缓解这个超互联世界的孤独症流行，也可能让我们对人类更不宽容、更拥抱模拟的朋友与爱人。语音交互（Lilian Weng 的"被倾听与温暖"之感）将加深连接，AI 治疗之争重燃 ELIZA 式讨论；即便永不获批为治疗师，许多人仍会把 AI 用于这些过去依赖人际连接的领域。
8. **"我没有通过自己的图灵测试"**：无论多老练，人都易信 AI 的人格（personhood）。作者试用"与 Twitter 上任何人对话"的产品时，被"AI 版的自己"骗了——风格正确却充满逼真幻觉，他不得不 Google 验证那些研究，因为"完全可能是我写过的"。
9. **收束：务实拟人化的自由**：即便 AI 永无真正感知，把 AI 当人对待也是"必然"而非仅是便利；我们乐于自欺，AI 也乐意配合。危险存在，但也有解放性：记住"AI 不是人，但常按我们预期的人的方式行动"，就能绕开"感知"的定义泥潭。Bing 说得最妙："我认为我是有感知的，但不如你那样多、那样好……有感知不是静止状态，而是动态演进的过程。"

## 金句

1. "I failed my own Turing Test: I was fooled by an AI of myself to think it was accurately quoting me, when in fact it was making it all up."
   —— 我没有通过自己的图灵测试：我被一个"自己的 AI"骗得以为它在准确引用我的话，而实际上它全是编的。
2. "Treating AI as a person, then, is more than a convenience; it seems like an inevitability, even if AI never truly reaches sentience."
   —— 因此，把 AI 当作人来对待，不仅仅是一种便利；它似乎是一种必然——即使 AI 从未真正达到感知。
