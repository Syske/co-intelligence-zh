## 本单元讲了什么

本单元先为上单元的"超级英雄做浓缩咖啡"案例收尾：Nightcrawler 传送到意大利向意式咖啡师学手艺、捎回现煮浓缩咖啡，猩红女巫用混沌魔法"凭空变出"惊艳浓缩咖啡，还有人提议做"连接全球咖啡大师的虚拟工作坊"——这些点子作为灵感来源很有趣，但仍需人类在回路中筛选；作者把这套方法搬进创业课堂，学生点子质量显著提升，落点是"让 AI 上桌是廉价的额外创新来源，也是新视角"。进入正题"将 AI 加入创作"，Mollick 提出一个反直觉观察：数量惊人的大量工作其实正是 AI 擅长的那种创造性工作——没有标准答案、容得下阐释、小错能被专家用户发现的场景（营销文案、绩效评语、战略备忘录）比比皆是，加上训练数据充足、写法套路化，AI 产出常常又快又好。MIT 经济学家 Noy 与 Zhang 的研究提供了硬证据：使用 ChatGPT 的职场写作者任务时间锐减 37%，人类评判的质量反而更高，且第一轮的低分组获益最多——AI 队友在缩小生产力不平等。接着作者把镜头转向"不似创造性的创造性"：编程是创造力与模式匹配的结合（微软研究 +55.8%），"意图编程"可能重塑 4640 亿美元的软件业，作者自称不会写代码却已让 AI 写了十几个程序；AI 也擅长数据总结，但幻觉如影随形——盖茨比植入科幻梗实验中，AI 找出两处、幻觉一处，还"误伤"了盖茨比宅邸 40 英亩地产的设定。芝加哥大学研究则展示了通用知识的威力：ChatGPT 毫无专门股市知识，却胜过金融机构花重金打造的专用模型，成为"未来股价波动的有力预测器"。准确率之争仍未决（JAMA 论文中 AI 回答的"共情力"近人类医生 10 倍），作者随后切入最深的艺术争议：Nick Cave 称 AI 歌词是"对人的存在状态的怪诞嘲弄"，宫崎峻称 AI 艺术"是对生命本身的侮辱"，AI 作品夺冠引发"艺术死了"之争——但作者认为结局更可能是创造力的复兴而非崩塌，因为 AI 训练自人类文化遗产，文化素养深的人最能榨取它的独特产出（作家最擅长提示 AI，"华盛顿有时光机"梗即为一例）；反讽的是，面对什么都能造的机器，大众仍默认创作熟悉的内容（星战、蜘蛛侠、名人大理石雕像）——"但 AI 能做的有趣事情多得多！"（页尾中断，续至单元 15）。

Scarlet Witch—Using her chaos magic, she could conjure up the most amazing cup of espresso from nothing. A new product could offer a "Surprise Me" feature where the machine randomly selects from a range of beans, grinds, and brew methods to create an unexpected and delicious espresso.

The results can be interesting as sources of inspiration (I like the idea of the virtual coffee workshops!) but still require a human in the loop to filter and select the best ideas. Yet it allows us to outsource some of the most difficult aspects of creativity. When I started requiring students to use these methods to generate ideas for start-ups in my entrepreneurship class, I found that the ideas' quality increased tremendously from the prior year. I encountered novel business ideas rather than seeing the same few ideas over and over again (better ways to order drinks at bars, companies that would store your stuff between semesters—they are students after all). Having the AI at the table was a cheap source of additional innovation and a new perspective.

**Adding AI to Creative Work**

Upon closer inspection, a surprisingly large amount of work is actually creative work in the form that AI is good at. Situations in which there is no right answer, where invention matters and small errors can be caught by expert users, abound. Marketing writing, performance reviews, strategic memos—all these are within the capability of AI because they have both room for interpretation and are relatively easily fact-checked. Plus, as many of these document types are well represented in the AI training data, and are rather formulaic in approach, AI results can often seem better than that of a human and can be produced faster as well.

We can see these results in a study by economists Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang from MIT, examining how ChatGPT could transform the way we work. The researchers asked the participants to write different types of documents based on their roles and scenarios. For example, the participants who were marketers had to create a press release for a fictional product; the participants who were grant writers had to craft a cover letter for a grant proposal; the participants who were managers and HR professionals had to compose a long email for the whole company on a delicate issue; the participants who were data analysts had to design an analysis plan in a code-notebook format; and the participants who were consultants had to produce a short report based on three given sources. Some were assigned to use AI and some were not. The results were nothing short of astonishing. Participants who used ChatGPT saw a dramatic reduction in their time on tasks, slashing it by a whopping 37 percent. Not only did they save time, but the quality of their work also increased as judged by other humans. These improvements were not limited to specific areas; the entire time distribution shifted to faster work, and the entire grade distribution shifted to higher quality. The study also showed that AI teammates helped reduce productivity inequality. Participants who scored lower on the first round without AI assistance benefited more from using ChatGPT, narrowing the gap between low and high scorers.

Even things that don't initially appear to be creative can be. AI works tremendously well as a coding assistant because writing software code combines elements of creativity with pattern matching. Again, early research suggests big impacts. When researchers from Microsoft assigned programmers to use AI, they found an increase of 55.8 percent in productivity for sample tasks. AI can even turn nonprogrammers into coders, of a sort. I can't write code in any modern language, but I have had AI write a dozen programs for me. The idea of programming by intent, by asking the AI to do something and having it create the code, is likely to have significant impacts in an industry whose workers earn a total of $464 billion a year. One fun, if less economically significant, impact: the lights in my office now flash in different colors when I yell "party"—the AI wrote the code to do that, walked me through setting up accounts with various cloud services companies to make the program work, and debugged problems when they occurred.

AI is also good at summarizing data since it is adept at finding themes and compressing information, though at the ever-present risk of error. As an example, I added tiny science fiction references to The Great Gatsby—in the middle of the text, Daisy refers to her iPhone, and one of Gatsby's gardeners uses a laser-powered lawn mower. I asked the AI to tell me if anything seemed unusual. It found both errors but also hallucinated a third (a mention of texting that was not there). Amusingly, it also pointed out what it found to be an unconvincing claim: There were 40 acres of land at Gatsby's mansion, unlikely in densely populated Long Island.

This ability to do high-quality analysis and summarization is not just useful for the topic of Gatsby's fictional real estate; it has real financial implications as well. A study by researchers at the University of Chicago used ChatGPT to analyze the conference-call transcripts of large companies, asking the AI to summarize the risks that companies faced. Risk obviously plays a big role in stock market returns, so financial firms have spent a lot of time and money using specialized, older forms of machine learning to try to identify the uncertainties associated with various corporations. ChatGPT, without any specialized stock market knowledge, tended to outperform these more specialized models, working as a "powerful predictor of future stock price volatility." In fact, it was the ability of the AI to apply more generalized knowledge of the world that made it such a good analyst, since it could put the risks discussed in conference calls into a larger context. The issue of hallucination was less important here, because the AI simply had to beat the best computer systems for accuracy, which it was able to do.

Of course, the unresolved question is whether AI is more or less accurate than humans, and whether its extended abilities to do creative, human work makes up for its errors. The trade-offs are often surprising. A paper published in the Journal of the American Medical Association: Internal Medicine asked ChatGPT-3.5 to answer medical questions from the internet, and had medical professionals evaluate both the AI's answers and an answer provided by a doctor. The AI was almost 10 times as likely to be rated as very empathetic than the results provided by the human, and 3.6 times as likely to be rated as providing good-quality information compared to human doctors. AIs can perform useful tasks that may not be considered traditionally creative work, and the coming months and years will likely see even more applications.

But what happens when AI touches on the most deeply human creative tasks—art? Artists have been reacting with alarm to the rapid encroachment of AI tools. Some of that concern is aesthetic. "A grotesque mockery of what it is to be human" is how famed musician Nick Cave described an AI attempt to create lyrics "in the style of a Nick Cave song." Animator Hayao Miyazaki called AI art "an insult to life itself." When one artist won a competition with an AI-generated piece, it caused an outcry, but the winning artist defended the AI's work. "Art is dead, dude. It's over. A.I. won. Humans lost."

The meaning of art is an old debate and one unlikely to be resolved in this book or any other. And the anxiety that artists face may soon be felt by many other professions as AI overlaps with their jobs. Yet this may turn out to be a reinvigoration of creativity and art rather than its collapse.

AI is trained on vast swaths of humanity's cultural heritage, so it can often be best wielded by people who have a knowledge of that heritage. To get the AI to do unique things, you need to understand parts of the culture more deeply than everyone else using the same AI systems. So now, in many ways, humanities majors can produce some of the most interesting "code." Writers are often the best at prompting AI for written material because they are skilled at describing the effects they want prose to create ("end on an ominous note," "make the tone increasingly frantic"). They are good editors, so they can provide instructions back to the AI ("make the second paragraph more vivid"). They can quickly run experiments with audiences and styles by knowing many examples of both ("make this like something in The New Yorker," "do this in the style of John McPhee"). And they can manipulate narrative to get the AI to think in the way they want. ChatGPT won't produce an interview between George Washington and Terry Gross, because such a scenario seems implausible. But if you convince it that George Washington might have had a time machine, it is happy to reply.

A similar phenomenon is happening in visual art. AI image generators have trained deeply on the past paintings and watercolors, architecture and photographs, fashion and historical images. Creating something interesting with AI requires you to invoke these connections to create a novel image. Given a machine that can make anything, we still default to what we know well.

## 中文翻译

**作为创造者的 AI —— 将AI加入创作（Adding AI to Creative Work）**

……咖啡师，并传送回来时捎上一杯现煮的浓缩咖啡。一款新产品可以把用户与世界各地的咖啡大师连接起来，参加线上的咖啡工作坊（virtual coffee workshops），获得地道的体验。猩红女巫（Scarlet Witch）——利用她的混沌魔法，她可以凭空变出一杯最惊艳的浓缩咖啡。一款新产品则可以提供"给我惊喜"（Surprise Me）功能：机器从多种咖啡豆、研磨方式和冲煮方法中随机挑选，创造出一杯出乎意料又美味的浓缩咖啡。

这些结果作为灵感的来源很有意思（我很喜欢线上咖啡工作坊这个点子！），但仍然需要一个人在回路中（a human in the loop）过滤并筛选出最好的点子。不过，它确实让我们得以把创意中最困难的一些方面外包出去。当我在创业课上开始要求学生用这些方法来生成创业点子时，我发现点子质量比上一年有了巨大提升。我见到的是新颖的商业创意，而不是翻来覆去那几个老点子（在酒吧点单的更好方式、学期之间帮你存东西的公司——毕竟他们是学生）。让 AI 上桌，是廉价的额外创新来源，也是一个全新的视角。

**将AI加入创作**

仔细审视之下，数量惊人的大量工作，其实正是 AI 擅长的那种形式的创造性工作。没有标准答案、发明创造至关重要、且小错能被专家用户发现的场景，比比皆是。营销文案、绩效评语、战略备忘录——这些都在 AI 的能力范围之内，因为它们既有阐释的空间，又相对容易核查事实。此外，由于这类文档中有许多在 AI 训练数据里占比很高，写法也相当套路化，AI 的产出往往看起来比人类写的更好，速度还更快。

我们可以在经济学家 Shakked Noy 和 Whitney Zhang（来自 MIT）的研究中看到这些结果，该研究考察了 ChatGPT 如何改变我们的工作方式。研究者让参与者根据自己的角色和场景，撰写不同类型的文档。例如，营销人员要为一款虚构产品写一篇新闻稿；拨款申请撰写人要写一封拨款申请书的附函；经理和人力资源专业人士要就一个敏感问题给全公司写一封长邮件；数据分析师要设计一份代码笔记本（code-notebook）格式的分析计划；顾问则要根据给定的三个来源写一份简短的报告。一部分人被分配使用 AI，另一部分人没有。结果堪称惊人。使用 ChatGPT 的参与者完成任务的时间大幅缩短，锐减了足足 37%。他们不仅省下了时间，工作质量（由其他人评判）也提高了。这些提升并不局限于特定领域：整个耗时分布都向更快的工作移动，整个评分分布都向更高的质量移动。研究还显示，AI 队友有助于减少生产力不平等（productivity inequality）。第一轮未使用 AI 时得分较低的参与者，从 ChatGPT 中获益更多，拉近了低分段与高分段之间的距离。

甚至那些起初看起来并不具创造性的事情，也可能具有创造性。AI 作为编程助手表现极其出色，因为编写软件代码融合了创造力与模式匹配（pattern matching）。同样，早期研究也表明影响巨大。微软的研究者让程序员使用 AI 时，发现示例任务的生产力提高了 55.8%。AI 甚至能把非程序员变成某种意义上的程序员。我不会写任何现代编程语言的代码，但我已经让 AI 为我写了十几个程序。"意图编程"（programming by intent）——让 AI 做点什么、由它来生成代码——在一个从业者年收入总计 4640 亿美元的行业里，很可能产生重大影响。一个有趣的（虽然经济意义没那么大的）影响：我办公室的灯现在会在我喊"派对"时闪烁不同颜色——AI 写了实现这个功能的代码，带我完成了在各种云服务商那里开通账号的流程让程序跑起来，还在出现问题时帮我调试。

AI 还擅长总结数据，因为它善于发现主题和压缩信息——尽管时刻存在出错的风险。举个例子，我在《了不起的盖茨比》（The Great Gatsby）里加入了微小的科幻梗：在文本中间，黛西提到了她的 iPhone，盖茨比的一位园丁用着激光动力割草机。我问 AI 有没有什么看起来不寻常的地方。它找出了两处错误，但还幻觉出了第三处（一段并不存在的"发短信"的描述）。有趣的是，它还指出了一处它认为不可信的说法：盖茨比宅邸有 40 英亩土地——在人口稠密的长岛，这不太可能。

这种高质量分析与总结的能力，不仅仅对盖茨比的虚构地产有用，它也有真金白银的财务意义。芝加哥大学的研究者用 ChatGPT 分析大公司的财报电话会议记录，让 AI 总结公司面临的风险。风险显然在股市回报中扮演重要角色，因此金融机构花了大量时间和金钱，使用更专门化的、较老形式的机器学习，试图识别与各家上市公司相关的不确定性。ChatGPT 没有任何专门的股市知识，却往往胜过这些更专门化的模型，成为"未来股价波动的有力预测器"（a powerful predictor of future stock price volatility）。事实上，正是 AI 运用更广义世界知识的能力，使它成为如此出色的分析师——因为它能把电话会议中讨论的风险放进更大的背景中。幻觉问题在这里没那么重要，因为 AI 只需要在准确率上击败最好的计算机系统，而它做到了。

当然，悬而未决的问题是：AI 究竟比人类更准还是更不准？它那被延展到创造性人类工作上的能力，能否抵消它的错误？权衡常常出人意料。《美国医学会杂志·内科学》（Journal of the American Medical Association: Internal Medicine）发表的一篇论文让 ChatGPT-3.5 回答网上的医学问题，并让医学专业人士同时评估 AI 的回答和一位医生提供的回答。AI 的回答被评为"非常有共情力"的可能性几乎是人类医生的 10 倍，被评为"信息质量好"的可能性是人类医生的 3.6 倍。AI 能够执行有用的任务，其中一些可能并不属于传统意义上的创造性工作，而未来数月乃至数年，很可能会出现更多应用。

但是，当 AI 触及最深刻的人类创造性任务——艺术——时，会发生什么？艺术家们一直在以警觉回应 AI 工具的快速侵蚀。这种担忧有些是美学层面的。著名音乐人 Nick Cave 这样描述一次 AI 试图创作"Nick Cave 风格"歌词的行为："这是对人的存在状态的怪诞嘲弄。"动画导演宫崎峻称 AI 艺术是"对生命本身的侮辱"。当一位艺术家凭借 AI 生成的作品赢得比赛时，引发了轩然大波，但获奖艺术家为 AI 的作品辩护："艺术死了，伙计。结束了。AI 赢了。人类输了。"

艺术的意义是一场古老的争论，不太可能在这本书或任何一本书中得到解决。而艺术家面临的焦虑，随着 AI 与各行各业的工作产生重叠，可能很快被许多其他职业感受到。然而，这最终可能是创造力与艺术的复兴（reinvigoration），而非其崩塌。

AI 在训练中吸收了人类文化遗产的广袤地带，因此它往往能被那些了解这份遗产的人发挥出最大威力。要让 AI 做出独特的东西，你需要比使用同一 AI 系统的其他人更深入地理解文化的某些部分。所以现在，在许多方面，人文学科专业的毕业生能够产出一些最有趣的"代码"。作家往往最擅长为文字材料提示 AI，因为他们善于描述自己希望文字产生的效果（"以不祥的音调结尾""让语气越来越狂躁"）。他们是优秀的编辑，因此能给 AI 提供反馈指令（"让第二段更生动"）。他们能通过了解大量的受众与风格范例，快速试验受众和风格（"写得像《纽约客》里的文章""用 John McPhee 的风格"）。他们还能操纵叙事，让 AI 按他们想要的方式思考。ChatGPT 不会创作一场 George Washington 和 Terry Gross 之间的访谈，因为这样的场景看起来不太可能发生。但如果你让它相信 George Washington 可能有一台时光机，它会很乐意作答。

类似的现象也发生在视觉艺术中。AI 图像生成器在过去的绘画与水彩、建筑与照片、时尚与历史影像上进行了深度训练。用 AI 创造有趣的图像，需要你唤起这些连接，生成新颖的画面。但大多数人在 AI 艺术工具上实际创作的东西，与此非常不同，也远没有那么雄心勃勃。有大量《星球大战》风格的艺术、大量电影明星的假照片、一些动漫、赛博朋克、大量超级英雄（尤其是蜘蛛侠），以及奇怪得很——大量的名人大理石雕像。给了一台什么都能造的机器，我们仍然默认涌向自己所熟知的东西。

但是 AI 能做的有趣事情多得多！AI 可以创作蜘蛛侠的大理石雕像，但它也能输出一个相当……

## 核心观点

1. **灵感来源 ≠ 可用产出，人类在回路中过滤**：超级英雄浓缩咖啡案例收尾——Nightcrawler 传送到意大利向意式咖啡师学手艺、捎回现煮浓缩咖啡；猩红女巫用混沌魔法"凭空变出"惊艳浓缩咖啡（对应"惊喜"功能：随机选豆、研磨、冲煮）；还有"连接全球咖啡大师的虚拟工作坊"。这些作为灵感来源有趣，但仍需"回路中的人"筛选最好的点子；真正的价值在于把创意中最困难的方面外包出去——创业课实验点子质量显著提升，"让 AI 上桌是廉价的额外创新来源，也是新视角"。
2. **大量普通工作其实是 AI 擅长的创造性工作**："没有标准答案、发明至关重要、小错能被专家用户发现"的场景比比皆是；营销文案、绩效评语、战略备忘录既有阐释空间又相对容易核查事实；训练数据充足 + 写法套路化 → AI 产出常显得比人类更好且更快。
3. **MIT 研究（Noy & Zhang）：时间 -37%，质量反升，差距缩小**：不同职业角色（营销/拨款撰写/经理与 HR/数据分析/顾问）按场景写作，使用 ChatGPT 者任务时间锐减 37%，人类评判的质量也更高；整个耗时分布与评分分布整体平移（而非少数人受益）；AI 队友"减少生产力不平等"——第一轮低分组获益更多。
4. **编程 = 创造力 + 模式匹配**：AI 是极好的编程助手；微软研究显示示例任务生产力 +55.8%；AI 甚至能把非程序员变成"某种意义上的"程序员；"意图编程"（programming by intent）可能深刻影响从业者年收入 4640 亿美元的行业；作者自述不会写任何现代语言代码，却已让 AI 写了十几个程序，连开通云服务账号、调试问题都靠 AI 带着走。
5. **数据总结是强项，幻觉如影随形**：AI 善于发现主题、压缩信息，但错误风险常在；盖茨比实验——植入两处科幻细节（黛西的 iPhone、激光动力割草机），AI 全部找出，却幻觉出第三处（不存在的"发短信"），还"误伤"了盖茨比宅邸 40 英亩土地的设定（人口稠密的长岛不太可能）。
6. **芝加哥大学研究：通用知识击败专用模型**：ChatGPT 无任何专门股市知识，分析财报电话会议记录、总结公司风险，胜过金融机构花重金打造的更专门化的旧式机器学习模型，成为"未来股价波动的有力预测器"；关键在 AI 能把电话会议中的风险放进更大的世界语境；幻觉问题在此不重要——AI 只需在准确率上击败最好的计算机系统即可。
7. **准确率之问未决，权衡出人意料**：AI 是否比人类更准、其错误能否被"能做更多创造性人类工作"抵消，仍未有定论；JAMA 内科学论文——ChatGPT-3.5 回答网络医学问题，被评"非常有共情力"的可能性近人类医生 10 倍、"信息质量好" 3.6 倍。
8. **艺术冲击：美学层面的反抗与"艺术已死"之争**：Nick Cave 称 AI 仿作是"对人的存在状态的怪诞嘲弄"；宫崎峻称 AI 艺术"是对生命本身的侮辱"；AI 作品夺冠引发众怒，获奖者辩护"艺术死了，伙计。AI 赢了。人类输了。"作者认为艺术意义之争不会有定论，但艺术家的焦虑将蔓延至其他职业——然而结局可能是创造力与艺术的复兴（reinvigoration）而非崩塌。
9. **文化素养 = AI 时代的新杠杆**：AI 训练自人类文化遗产的广袤地带，理解文化越深，越能让 AI 产出独特的东西；人文学科专业者现在能产出最有趣的"代码"；作家最擅长提示 AI 的四项技能——描述想要的效果（"以不祥的音调结尾"）、编辑反馈（"让第二段更生动"）、受众与风格实验（"像《纽约客》"、"用 John McPhee 的风格"）、操纵叙事（"华盛顿有时光机"梗）。
10. **AI 艺术的反讽：面对万能机器，我们仍默认熟悉**：AI 图像生成器深度训练于绘画、水彩、建筑、摄影、时尚与历史影像，创造有趣的图像需唤起这些连接；但大众实际创作的却是星战同人、明星假照片、动漫、赛博朋克、超级英雄（尤其蜘蛛侠）和大量名人大理石雕像——"给了一台什么都能造的机器，我们仍然默认涌向自己所熟知的东西"；作者疾呼"但 AI 能做的有趣事情多得多！"（页尾中断，续至单元 15）。

## 金句

1. "Upon closer inspection, a surprisingly large amount of work is actually creative work in the form that AI is good at."
   —— 仔细审视之下，数量惊人的大量工作，其实正是 AI 擅长的那种形式的创造性工作。
2. "Having the AI at the table was a cheap source of additional innovation and a new perspective."
   —— 让 AI 上桌，是廉价的额外创新来源，也是一个全新的视角。
3. "Given a machine that can make anything, we still default to what we know well."
   —— 给了一台什么都能造的机器，我们仍然默认涌向自己所熟知的东西。
